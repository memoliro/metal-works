How the elbow gore works

  1. Cut 2 end gores and 3 middle gores for the piece count you set.
  2. The pattern is drawn with the outside of the bend (heel) at the centre of the sheet and the throat at both edges.
  3. Roll each gore to the cladding diameter, overlap the lap strip, and work from one tangent end toward the other, riveting as you go.
Ordinates — distance around branch vs. cut depth from the reference edge

Using the template

  1. Print 1:1, tape the tiles together, and measure the 100 mm scale bar on a tile before trusting it.
  2. Wrap the template around the branch with the straight reference edge square to the pipe, seam the edges together, and mark the curve.
  3. The two low points of the curve sit on the header's centreline sides; the high points face along the header. Cut, then dress to fit.

    Steel pipe sizes — ASME B36.10 (American)

    Outside diameter is the same for every schedule at a given NPS. Wall and bore shown for Schedule 40 (STD weight up to 10″).

    NPSOD (in)OD (mm)Sch 40 wall (mm)Bore / ID (mm)
    DNNPS equiv.OD EN 10220 (mm)OD ASME (mm)

    EN 10220 Series 1 outside diameters (aligned with ISO 4200). DN is a label, not a measurement — DN50 pipe is 60.3 mm across, not 50. Highlighted rows are where the European OD differs from the ASME OD for the same nominal size — most importantly DN65: 76.1 mm (EN) vs 73.0 mm (ASME). Pipe, fittings and cladding must all follow one standard at those sizes. EN 10220 also tabulates wall thicknesses and masses per unit length, but there is no single European “Schedule 40” wall, so no wall column is shown.

    Use loads that OD into the cladding tool's Pipe OD — or pick NPS / DN sizes directly from the preset lists in the cladding and saddle tools. For cladding, add 2 × insulation thickness to the OD. For saddles, use the OD of both pipes exactly as they are.
